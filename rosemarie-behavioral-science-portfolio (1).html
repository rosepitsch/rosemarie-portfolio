How I work

From behavioral diagnosis to content, experiments, and outcomes.

These patterns show up across different kinds of work, whether I am supporting patients, clinicians, or product teams.

Behavioral diagnosis
Behavioral design exercise for a neighborhood platform

Start with the choice environment, not just the interface.

  • I map the end-to-end path a user takes to complete a behavior, looking for decision points, drop-offs, and the moment intent is most likely to fail.
  • In one exercise, that meant tracing the path from a positive offline experience with a local business to the moment a neighbor tries to recommend that business digitally and gets stuck.
  • I distinguish between upstream choice-architecture problems and downstream friction, then prioritize the change that reshapes the initial decision rather than only smoothing later steps.
Co-design and content systems
Digital sexual health program at an academic research center

Use research and co-design to shape content and flows.

  • I work with advisory boards, subject-matter experts, and end users to understand real experiences, language, and mental models before locking content.
  • On a digital sexual health project for young adults, that included partnering with a youth advisory board to define session themes, key messages, and example content and scenarios.
  • I turn those inputs into structured content systems—session outlines, activities, and decision-tree style interactions—that support both education and behavior change.
Evaluation and iteration
Digital interventions and content pilots

Design measurement into the work from the start.

  • I prefer to build evaluation into interventions rather than treat it as an afterthought.
  • In past projects, that has meant using randomized and quasi-experimental designs, usability sessions, and qualitative feedback to understand not only whether something worked, but why.
  • Those findings then feed back into content, flows, and stakeholder recommendations, so the work becomes more effective over time.
